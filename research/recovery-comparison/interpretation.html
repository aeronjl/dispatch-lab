What this changes

The release makes service recovery an observable, bounded process: completing work, confirming capacity and producing methane are separate outcomes. A repair receipt cannot stand in for a successful operating test. Conversely, useful degraded operation can resume before the observer has established full nameplate capacity.

The strongest qualification result is also a warning about policy stability. All null dispatch/state operands match exactly across modes and repeats. Outside the null case, the largest same-seed numerical-repeat difference is 115.55 kg of methane. In that risk-aware run, ambiguous observations at H15 reduced the capacity estimate from 450 to about 126.9 kW, below the 135 kW operating minimum. Verification expired overnight and the physically healthy electrolyser remained idle. There was no confirmed incident, so the zero false-alarm count does not describe this harmful uncertain derating.

Inspect the original hourly observations and estimates. This is a post-hoc counterexample, not an isolated causal test of risk preferences or the first point of numerical divergence.

The short recovery window exposes a consequential scheduling weakness. A repair near sunset can finish successfully, yet its verification window can expire before enough electricity is available for consecutive load tests. The controller then escalates rather than silently declaring recovery or endlessly renewing a deadline. This is a visible limit of the policy, not evidence that the physical repair failed.

The 24-hour sensitivity produces methane in six of nine cases, compared with none in the matched 12-hour damage cases; no case confirms full nameplate capacity. Its successful-output cases produce between 24.55 and 74.52 kg. The preselected seed-7 timeline remains unsuccessful and includes additional repair in the longer-window case. It is retained to show that a longer deadline is not sufficient by itself.

Service exposure also depends on the controller. Deferring uncertain cleaning can avoid energy, interruption and support costs, while leaving little new evidence about cleaning duration. The report therefore retains accepted work, actual activity, human visits and ending resources alongside production and costs. No mode is declared a general winner from these short cases.

In the main normal-service cases, the risk-aware arm has a mean assumed operating contribution of about €62, compared with about −€122 for the other modes. But it uses service fallback in 99 of 144 intervals and leaves mean modelled surface transmission at 80.54%, versus 85.02% for the other modes. Its apparent short-period cost advantage mixes avoided work, unresolved planning and a worse ending surface condition. It is not evidence of an optimal risk policy or a durable economic advantage.

The earlier broad run found a real model inconsistency: random job duration changed brush consumption without changing optical treatment at the same rate. The corrected release reconciles both against the same treated area. The original failed result remains failed and available in the programme history.

Where the evidence points next

  1. Fix the interaction between ambiguous observations and recovery scheduling. A single uncertain update can move the estimate below the minimum operating load and remove the next informative operating opportunity. Test uncertainty/covariance handling and deliberate re-observation around that boundary. Plan verification before committing work, accounting for daylight, stored energy, gas headroom and progressively larger load tests. Separate initial diagnosis from post-mission commissioning deadlines, retaining finite escalation and reporting useful production as well as full-capacity confirmation.
  2. Stabilise the comparison before expanding the controller family. Retain computation repeats and solver budgets alongside environmental seeds. Examine candidate truncation, service fallbacks and ending resources; then extend the most informative service exposures and time windows. A larger learned policy comparison would currently mix these effects with learning.
  3. Obtain identified operational observations. Repeated jobs on the same equipment are needed to distinguish persistent differences from job variation. Record unsuccessful and censored work, nominal recipes, weather/access conditions, support interruptions and observation availability. The new calibration interface can consume those data; synthetic fitting does not establish real distributions or repair capabilities.
  4. Extend contingent planning only with qualified branches. The implemented example permits charging after a nominated retrieval of an already observed stranded robot. Planning for a possible future interruption still needs explicit branches, observation boundaries, carrier/crew constraints and independent checks. It is not yet a general fleet-recovery planner.
  5. Bring first use under the latency budget. The repeated-interaction p95 is below 200 ms, including with batch activity, but cold interactions still exceed it. Isolate component initialisation and transport scheduling before adding prewarming or further caching, and measure the first interaction as its own acceptance condition.

These are follow-on steps, not a resumption of the separately paused fourteen-family hardware programme. The current evidence does not calibrate fault incidence, repair success, weather-error tails or annual return on service investment.
