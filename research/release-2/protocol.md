# Release 2 experiment protocol

This programme evaluates the new lifecycle mechanisms. It does not retrain a
controller or claim calibrated robot capability. Historical stage-3 numerical
repetitions are separate from these new experiments.

## Matched comparisons

- Nine preserved ERA5 weeks: London, Seville and Copenhagen, starting 10 January,
  10 April and 10 July 2025. Four maintenance rules, two sensor seeds: 72 cases.
  The periodic rule is due at H168; the short windows end before its first work
  interval. That rule is an unexposed control in the weekly cases, not an active
  preventive-maintenance comparison. The annual and teaching cases exercise due dates.
  The forecast is explicitly previous-complete-day persistence. Reanalysis is
  not site measurement and persistence is not an archived operational forecast.
- One full continuous London 2025 environment: four rules, commissioning of a
  declared previously used plant, initial stack exposure 38,000 operating hours
  and PV exposure two years. Stocks, condition, work and costs cross 168-hour
  checkpoint boundaries without reset. The annual periodic rule is every 4,380
  hours, a design choice. This supports one-year cash accounting, not an
  extrapolated twenty-year profitability claim.
- Seville July sensitivity cases: lower rate/longer work; higher rate/shorter work;
  access, reference and communications outage with delayed stock; failed work;
  missing condition channel; lower power/storage; and greater CO₂ supply. All four
  rules see the same disclosed scenario and seed: 28 cases. Bundled rate/duration
  challenges are not individual causal effects or a probability distribution.
- Twelve 72-hour cases use the saved Seville ECMWF issue archive: four maintenance
  rules with each of the three process objectives. These are separate from the
  persistence design studies. Time limits, feasible fallbacks and incomplete runs
  are retained; no policy is required to win.

The short-week condition fixture starts at 45,000 PEM operating hours and twenty
PV years. This deliberately exposes replacement decisions near/beyond the scoped
reference criterion. It is an extrapolative challenge, not a claim about the
age distribution or safe life of real equipment. No-maintenance uses the same
opening spare stock and costs as the other rules. A later sensitivity can ask a
separate procurement-design question.

## Questions and reporting boundaries

1. Does reserved capacity/energy change later production after commissioned
   capacity arrives? Report acceptance and departure separately.
2. When does extra efficiency justify lost production and consumed replacement
   stock? Report actual cash, allocated cost and operating contribution separately.
3. Does maintenance only appear beneficial at a chosen boundary? Inspect the
   continuous trajectory at 24, 72 and 168 hours and longer annual checkpoints;
   retain all energy/feedstock/thermal inventories, condition, spares and work.
4. Which observations support confirmation? Procedures, readings and physical
   outcomes are different records. Missing observations cannot establish recovery.
5. Do feedstock or thermal bottlenecks hide the value of a better solar array,
   stack or intervention? Compare the explicit design changes, not only a rank.

Every study is created through Sites and retains its source capsule, original
inputs, weather identities and per-case chronology. Cancellation stops at a
committed boundary; resumption uses the same source. New parameter changes or
numerical repetitions require new editions. Saved source hashes bind findings;
new application versions do not inherit their qualification automatically.

Independent checks reconcile hourly plant balances, declared lifecycle clocks,
condition observations, stock and cash. Full unit/browser/legacy/offline checks
qualify the implementation separately. Annual field realism, empirical sensor
quality, service rates, quotations and degradation calibration remain evidence gaps.
