The programme supports continued controller engineering, but not a claim of autonomous site operation. Planning can improve production under matched service rules. The current weather-aware recovery deadline can nevertheless prevent the controller from confirming a physically successful repair. Human work, constrained observation and numerical budgets remain material parts of the result.

1. Separate production objectives from the service policy

The original Seville ranking was 399.4 kg for Greedy, 260.7 kg for methane MPC and 184.0 kg for economic MPC over 72 hours. In the new comparison that holds the local service and recovery rules constant, the two repetitions produce 318.8–320.9 kg for Greedy, 361.3–374.2 kg for methane MPC and 342.9–348.4 kg for economic MPC. All confirm recovery by hour 38. Within this group, only the process objective differs in the explicit controller policy. The result establishes a conditional advantage in this fixture; it does not establish that MPC always wins.

The original-package repeats also separate planning horizon from solver budget. A six-hour methane plan produces 268.1 kg with either 0.1 or 1 second per solve. At 24 hours, 0.1-second repeats produce 284.7 and 299.0 kg and use fallbacks; one-second repeats both produce 370.1 kg without fallbacks. The longer horizon changes the repair timing from hour 42 to hour 14. More solve time alone does not repair a short information horizon, and the same output total does not imply an identical physical trace.

The economic objective gives a different trade-off in the common-service comparison: it produces less methane but has a less negative assumed contribution (€−940.1 to €−938.4) than methane MPC (€−1,022.0 to €−1,016.3). All remain negative over the three-day incident window. Ending gas and battery inventories are shown separately, without speculative sale proceeds. These numbers do not annualise a forced incident or establish project profitability.

2. A first successful test is not full recovery

In the matched capacity-loss cases, every service restores physical capacity at hour 20. With the version-4 weather deadline, neither ambiguity rule confirms full recovery within 72 hours. Retaining the estimate with the version-3 fixed deadline confirms recovery at hour 41 for all three seeds and produces 374.0–393.5 kg, compared with 325.1–330.6 kg for retention with the weather deadline. These are matched scheduler alternatives under the same observations and physical scenario.

The recorded seed-7 sequence explains the failure. At hour 20 the controller estimates 218.4 kW of a 450 kW electrolyser. A successful pair of tests raises that estimate to 263.4 kW. The weather scheduler has allotted four hours for recovery, although the configured 45 kW increments require six confirmed increases to reach nameplate. Even optimistically crediting an initial observation, at least eleven intervals are required. The controller escalates at hour 24 and never performs the remaining increases. The compact report includes a necessary test-count check for every recorded version-4 opening; it is not a proof that power or weather would permit all the tests.

The next controller change should separate a test appointment from the overall recovery deadline. Keep an immutable outer bound, schedule the next informative test within it, and advance through confirmed increases. Qualify that new policy on fresh matched repetitions before promoting it. Version 4 remains unchanged in this programme, preserving the failure and the original evidence.

3. Recovery, sensor isolation and physical repair are different outcomes

All nine flow-bias ablation cases isolate the inconsistent flow channel by completed interval 12. Under the same bias, the reduction rule produces 350.3–400.0 kg, compared with 399.3–400.0 kg for retention with the weather deadline. Reducing capacity on ambiguous evidence can impose a production penalty even when capacity itself is intact. Failed-remedy cases never recover physical capacity and remain visible in the results. The six uncertainty-free repeated cases report 400.0 kg and no false alarms or human visits; the trace-identity table separately tests numerical repeatability.

The capacity-loss, failed-remedy and flow-bias ablations invoke a human intervention; the flow-bias reduction case at seed 29 invokes two visits. This is autonomous detection, scheduling and bounded verification around assumed human service, not robotic physical repair. Zero false alarms in this bounded programme is not a measured false-alarm rate for an operating plant.

4. Measure the assumptions that change the decision

A 24-hour crew mobilisation delay moves Greedy's confirmation from hours 36–38 to hour 63 and reduces its methane by 30.6–80.6 kg across the three seeds. For the MPC package, which already has the deadline limitation, its production difference is −27.7 to +4.8 kg and full recovery remains unconfirmed. The value of faster service cannot be separated from the controller's ability to use and verify that service.

Doubling reactor thermal capacity changes methane by −50.9 to +10.5 kg for Greedy and −15.3 to +12.5 kg for the MPC package. Multiplying archived forecast irradiance by 1.5 changes output in both directions, with reductions as large as 53.8 kg. Halving initial battery inventory can also increase or decrease output. These are nonlinear, conditional trajectories with numerical limitations—not engineering response curves, calibrated uncertainty distributions or reasons to prefer smaller storage. Repeat borderline comparisons with a larger numerical budget before attributing small differences to a physical mechanism.

The practical evidence priorities are therefore the post-service sensor chain and test requirements, cause-specific remedy capability, mobilisation and spare availability, and measured reactor warm-up/cool-down. Real DC output and issue-time forecasts are needed to establish the power available for recovery. Vendor service and equipment quotations remain necessary for economic conclusions. This programme does not fabricate calibration observations or purchase evidence.

Feedstock is another decisive comparison boundary. The three-day fixture starts with 500 kg CO₂ and receives two 300 kg deliveries. At 2.75 kg CO₂ per kg methane, even perfect operation cannot exceed 400 kg methane. Seven-day supply similarly caps output at about 836.4 kg. Several cases reach these limits. Equal methane can therefore hide differences in energy use, recovery and ending hydrogen; additional solar cannot remove a CO₂ constraint. CO₂ delivery capability and logistics belong in the next evidence pass.

5. Seasonal exposure is a qualification challenge, not a site ranking

The eighteen seven-day cases complete 3,024 operating intervals across the nine site/month combinations. Greedy produces more methane than the tested MPC package in each pair, but these comparisons use different service policies and a 0.2-second MPC budget. MPC records time-limited solves and fallbacks in every seasonal case. Some output differences are small; this is not a stable ranking of planning methods or candidate sites.

Both packages physically restore the asset in every seasonal case. Greedy confirms full recovery in seven of nine windows; the January London and Copenhagen windows end without confirmation. The version-4 MPC package confirms none of the nine. London January produces 144.9 kg with Greedy and 74.9 kg with MPC; London July produces 835.5 and 829.1 kg. The winter challenge is therefore not just a smaller annual solar total: useful operation, diagnosis, service timing and recovery opportunities interact within the window.

These are already-used 2025 reanalysis windows with causal previous-day persistence forecasts, one event seed and one numerical execution per package. They do not estimate real fault incidence, unseen-year reliability or deployment profitability. The nine pairs remain available individually, including ending inventories, human work and solver limitations.
