# Coordinating field work with production

`coordinated-services/1` and `/2` are opt-in operational policies. Experiment setup → Field operations exposes them with their assumptions. They require fractional service execution, finite logistics and complete activity-based service prices. Version 2 additionally requires enabled visit bundling. Existing archives and default runs retain the local rule. Greedy remains local; coordination applies to methane or economic MPC. The original plant illustration is unchanged.

## Information and action boundaries

Each hourly decision processes observations before requesting candidates. The controller sees the estimated plant state/capacity, eligible forecast, public service status, current resource ledger and recorded cost assumptions. It receives no injected cause, successful-repair draw, future recovery time or later weather. Registered recipes supply work duration, tools, energy, stock and isolation. The current ideal surface observation and raw solar forecast supply conditional cleaning benefits before clipping.

The search compares retaining accepted commitments with early, middle and late feasible starts for queued requests. The candidate budget and each production solve have distinct time limits. Version 1 considers one new request per candidate and requires its entire procedure and return to fit the horizon. Accepted longer commitments remain visible. It conservatively requires current hardware and stock readiness even for a later departure; it does not promise to repair or restock an unavailable asset beforehand.

Current dock power and process dispatch are solved together. Shared occupancy, return reserves, beginning-of-hour robot use, end-of-hour charge receipt, forecast service peaks and plant electrical balance are all enforced. Registered current recipes are checked again during acceptance; actual work may still be interrupted by subsequent conditions. Extra dock usage and mission costs use the same parts/usage allowance, without charging fixed ownership or service electricity twice.

## Obligations are not erased by waiting

Essential requests receive a fixed deadline at their original creation time. Optional cleaning and routine service do not receive that priority in this version. Candidate ordering first favours progress on due essential work, then its earlier deadline, then the declared process objective and incremental service cost. This operating priority is an assumption, not a calibrated monetary value of avoiding a fault. Procedure completion by a deadline includes return; confirmation of restored plant capability remains a separate observed event.

A missed deadline stays missed. A repair interrupted before completion may create a distinct attempt after the configured wait, with a new response lead and resource requirements. It retains its original obligation and deadline. Attempt exhaustion records an escalation requirement. Completed-but-unverified work is not automatically declared a failed repair. A stranded crew or robot remains unavailable: a retry request cannot create a return, a spare part or successful restoration. The optional [interrupted-crew return](crew-return.md) follows the remaining recorded travel legs, consumes crew time and skips unfinished work. Emergency rescue and damaged service vehicles remain outside that mechanism.

Observed low robot inventory creates an absolute charging deadline for the declared reserve or a known mission's need. Replanning may tighten the amount but cannot postpone the deadline. An overdue target asks for next-interval catch-up while retaining the missed original boundary. A target above installed capacity is explicitly infeasible. End-of-horizon energy, supplies and outstanding obligations remain recorded; none receives speculative resale value.

If no combined incumbent can progress due essential work, the fallback attempts one currently feasible essential request plus the existing surplus-charging rule. The process planner then solves against accepted demands. The fallback is recorded; it does not invent repair benefit. Optional cleaning cannot cancel an essential repair through a newly bundled visit, because this version creates separate new visits. The local baseline retains its original bundling.

## What is recorded

Every controlled decision stores its policy, original inputs and identity; evaluated, rejected, unresolved and omitted candidates; conditional process/service forecasts; requested charging; original obligations and energy targets; selected action or fallback. The services utility reveals this record without adding text to the main simulation. Source archives and the existing trace/export interfaces retain the same data. The ordinary component what-ifs keep the original service forecast fixed; changing service choices themselves remains a separate required workflow.

The `field-coordination` Studies protocol compares local methane MPC, coordinated methane MPC and coordinated economic MPC. Hardware and exogenous inputs are matched. Normal soiling, persistent damage, optimistic forecasts and charger failure each retain their unsuccessful and incomplete outcomes. Smoke checks test transport/accounting; three-seed reference and longer storage/clipping sensitivity cases test consequences. This is a package comparison: timing, demand prediction, charging, priority, retries and separate visits change together. It cannot isolate the value of one mechanism.

## Scope of current checks

Tests cover policy/configuration preservation; observation-only inputs and future-fault noninterference; original deadlines and retry budgets; impossible energy targets; cancellation before acceptance; solver fallback; mission/dock contention; actual energy and cost reconciliation; public decision rendering; and whole-run independent physical/service audits. A warm-plant charging fixture separately checks that delayed charging can preserve a short methane opportunity. Numerical checks are not empirical reliability evidence.

The complete stage-4 gate still requires observation-contingent remedies, failure/risk scenarios, targeted service alternatives and interpreted matched studies. Shared-visit proposals alone do not complete that gate. The broader six-stage goal also retains the remaining hardware, deep service essays, commissioning, degradation, predictive maintenance and held-out learning work.

## Shared visits in version 2

The same priority-ordered queue supplies at most eight currently compatible crew requests. The proposal family consists of contiguous groups of two through the configured maximum visit size, keeping their queue order. Each group offers early, middle and late feasible departure windows. Single and shared candidates are interleaved under the candidate limit. This does not search every subset, route permutation or future request. Requests excluded by the pool bound, candidates excluded by the solver-count bound, and rejected feasibility conditions are recorded separately from evaluated results.

Composition keeps original request evidence and the current observation context. It removes repeated home journeys, adds declared transfers and retains each procedure and verification stage. The whole crew/tool envelope, finite labour, all current kits, response lead, working shift and return are checked before acceptance. Every member must satisfy the response lead by departure, and every due obligation uses the whole visit return as its deadline boundary. Replenishment within the same candidate cannot pay for an initially unavailable part. An occupied or stranded crew cannot be promised available at a later start. One candidate can reserve one crew journey, which may include several jobs. A second separate departure requires a later decision after the first return is observed. The projection rejects unsupported multiple departures instead of reporting a feasible plan that execution would only partly accept.

The demand, charging and process interfaces use the composed itinerary. Service pricing counts one departure while retaining each job's labour, supplies and applicable wear. Candidate pricing `/2` also corrects a prior conservative overclassification: the registered routine-service procedure consumes a kit but does not deliver stock into a destination. Unknown or actual stock-delivery effects still require a disposition model. This correction can change candidate eligibility even with service policy version 1; original archives preserve the old source and decisions. A source-bound numerical rerun must report any differences.

Acceptance rebuilds the recipes and rechecks current resources. Each shared visit is atomic: either all its reservations are accepted or none are. The executive retains separate effects, spent materials, measurements and acceptance for each job. A visit interruption can strand the crew and stop later jobs, including essential work; version 2's conditional successful-continuation objective does not quantify that risk. That limitation motivates the remaining scenario and contingent-remedy work.

The new planning interfaces are `crew-visit-planning/1`, `service-process-demand-coupling/4`, `service-work-charge-controller/2` and `service-selection-port/3`. Older no-group interface shapes remain available. Independent recorded checks bind selected group order, departure and return to the accepted itinerary, and require explicit authorization for future visit departures.

## Pricing inputs and bounded search time

The first twelve-case smoke edition exposed a history-hashing bottleneck: a H12 prefix contained about 40.9 MB of JSON, and repeatedly hashing it exhausted the one-second comparison budget before some essential jobs were considered. Its source and interpreted report are retained as pre-optimisation evidence.

The coordinator now creates `service-cost-input-projection/1` once per decision. It copies the recorded service fields consumed by the accounting interface, preserving every original row and event index. Before using it, the implementation reconciles all quantities, source references, installed-asset intervals and stock operands against the complete prefix. A changed accounting dependency fails this check instead of silently losing an operand. Candidate price identities bind this economic input projection; they are explicitly not hashes of the entire physical trace. The full original trace remains in the archive.

This reduces repeated serialization without changing the declared cost model. More candidates can consequently fit within a time budget, so actual control decisions can change. A new study edition must evaluate that behavioural consequence; equality of accounting inputs alone does not establish equal dispatch or better outcomes.
