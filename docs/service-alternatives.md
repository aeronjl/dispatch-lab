# Original-information service alternatives

This stage-4 increment compares a targeted service choice with a recalculated baseline. Both use the original decision's estimated plant state, eligible forecast, observed service state and dispatch assumptions. A completed run is never changed. The recorded original prediction remains a third, explicitly labelled reference: a changed implementation, solver termination or incumbent can change a recalculation independently of the user's alternative.

## Information boundary

New fractional-service runs capture `service-planning-snapshot/2` after observations and work-request preparation, before new work is accepted. The snapshot contains current stocks, reservations, mission cursors, compatible registered recipes, readiness feedback, original observation context, and already observed optical treatments. Version 2 additionally saves bounded compatible procedure choices; version 1 still loads without inventing those choices. Service definitions are deduplicated by catalogue identity once per run. Process inputs are stored separately under `service-process-planning-inputs/1` before the selected service schedule changes the process forecast.

The planning adapter has no effect port and cannot submit or execute work. It does not receive future weather, injected fault schedules, private outcome variates, later inspection results or the physical success of a future repair. Original current charging is a requested action; its predicted receipt is conditional. Existing accepted commitments retain their observed cursors.

The worker receives `service-alternative-inputs/1`, containing this selected context and the exact earlier service-cost operands. The complete run and future rows are not sent to it. Dispatch cost identities are checked against original configuration, including the historical short process-price hash convention. Repriced reports are not calculation inputs. Original source and current replanner identities are exposed; changed source is labelled as current-model recalculation.

Older archives without original snapshots remain readable. Their service alternative returns an explicit unavailable result. Current documentation and recorded execution remain accessible; later outcomes are never used to reconstruct missing original planning evidence.

## Current supported changes

- Postpone work newly selected at the chosen decision by an explicit positive duration. Selecting one job from a shared visit moves the complete itinerary. Prior commitments and other selections remain fixed. A delay beyond the same forecast horizon, a crew-shift conflict, insufficient stock or blocked access stays infeasible.
- Defer an eligible cleaning job through the same schedule interface. Existing optical-soiling predictions use the saved untreated forecast and observed surface state. No new physical cleaning mechanism is introduced.
- Change a newly selected contact-reading or section-cleaning procedure to a compatible alternative installed at the original decision. Fixed/rover readers retain their own channels and the shared contact's error boundary; inspection earns no invented finding or repair benefit. Resident/portable cleaners retain their distinct coverage, loose/adhered-fouling removal, brush, water, electrical and crew requirements. The target, original request/evidence, departure, other work and energy commitments remain. Shared journeys are rebuilt and may be incompatible with the new actor; no automatic split or rescheduling occurs. Installed but unavailable choices retain their original restriction. Resource shortages remain infeasible. Missing hardware is not introduced by a what-if.
- Raise or add a robot-energy target for a decision that originally used joint service/production planning. Original target deadlines and minimum energy cannot be relaxed. Capacity and charging-power violations do not clamp the user's target.

Greedy/local-service scheduling retains its original current charge in both schedules and continues with the original process objective. A reserve comparison is currently unavailable for a local-service decision: adding an optimizer would also change its policy. Joint decisions retain their original absolute charging targets and original methane/economic objective. Comparison solvers retain the configured per-solve limit, validate incumbents and do not invent a fallback result. The recorded controller's original fallback is displayed separately.

New work must complete, including the whole shared return, within the original horizon. Prior accepted work may continue beyond it and remains listed. Conditional service inventories include projected consumption and charging; supply acceptance, future repair success and unknown inspection findings are not credited. Unselected work, original deadlines and terminal obligations remain visible.

## Economic reconciliation

Predicted service cost is the combined prefix-plus-continuation decision-cost pool less the already incurred prefix pool, plus any additional optimized dock usage. This retains the existing treatment of replaceable parts and usage allowances. Fixed current charging is included in its conditional continuation. Fixed ownership and sunk expenditure are excluded. Process electricity and lost methane are already represented physically and are not charged again as a duplicate service penalty. Ending inventories receive no resale proceeds.

Candidate pricing version 3 distinguishes registered dock charging from a purchased-material delivery: its activity and input electricity are known under the continuation assumption, while delivered battery energy is checked separately. Missing material acceptance is still a condition for actual supply procedures. This corrects a formerly undefined cost for fixed current charging and can change candidate eligibility; earlier decisions and their pricing identities remain recorded. A comparison is marked incomplete when either physical predictions or applicable decision costs are unresolved. Available physical predictions remain visible alongside undefined costs.

For the independent routine-service fixture, a two-job shared visit costs €530: one €300 callout, 1.5 crew-hours at €80, 0.5 vehicle-hours at €20 and two €50 kits. Moving the itinerary does not create another callout or consume another kit. The fixture assumes no maintenance-induced extension of life or recovery of production.

## Interaction and preservation

The comparison opens from the revealed Site services pane and pauses playback. A small server description provides the eligible choices; the browser never receives the full planning packet for rendering. Controls use explicit calculation, progress and cancellation. Changed inputs leave any previous result visibly labelled as belonging to previous inputs.

Each request belongs to a capability token, run, controller, decision hour and client generation. Cancel-before-start tombstones prevent out-of-order requests from reviving a cancelled generation. Polling and cancellation check complete selection ownership. A later generation stops the earlier worker; changed inputs cannot reuse its job identity. Two service comparison workers may run concurrently, each with single-thread numerical libraries and a separate wall-time limit. App shutdown cleans up workers. The original live simulation and other workspaces retain their presentation and artwork.

New reproduction playback includes the interface but explicitly requires a restored application for live recalculation. Opening saved playback does not contact a calculation service.

## Remaining completion gates

This is an increment within the six-stage programme. The compatible-procedure comparison covers the two explicitly implemented contact/section families, not arbitrary substitute repairs. Inspect-before-repair requires observation-contingent remedies and a declared current belief; a cheaper inspection with no subsequent change in action does not establish information value. Local-policy reserve alternatives and preservation of scheduled recovery-test commitments also remain required. The current adapter refuses decisions with scheduled or immediate recovery probes rather than dropping their constraints. Observed satisfaction of previous work remains distinct from predicted completion and future verification. Risk scenarios, service-specific Model essays, complete recorded lineage, broader comparative studies and all later hardware/learning stages remain open.

Focused checks cover snapshot round trips, active commitment cursors, dry/wet optical forecast equivalence, original-information separation, pricing identity checks, shared-visit postponement, fixed and optimized charging, infeasible inputs, missing original contexts, isolated workers and cancellation ownership. Browser checks separately exercise rendering, narrow screens and stale-response behaviour; their mocked paired values are UI fixtures, not physical research evidence. Release verification and source-specific artifacts must be recorded after the remaining checks pass.

The first checked service-alternative implementation (`99bcfc16…`) passed 726 Python tests, 64 Node checks, five browser checks and a real saved-run UI walkthrough. Its eight-hour example passes 1,499 independent archive checks; the complete JSON comparison reproduces under captured source. A 24-hour delay is preserved as infeasible. These artifacts are retained under `build/services/alternatives/recorded/` and `checked/` with their full source identifiers.

Release startup exposed a separate Gradio compatibility issue: the existing main archive's first localhost HEAD response took 3.67 seconds, exceeding the dependency's hardcoded three-second check. `methane.startup.launch_local` allows ten seconds for that actual local HTTP response, restores the dependency hook before serving, propagates failed readiness and never enables public sharing. Non-loopback/HTTPS checks keep the dependency's original behaviour. This does not accelerate model computation or change dispatch; it is a bounded cold-start allowance. The compatibility hook should be removed if Gradio provides a public readiness-timeout option.
