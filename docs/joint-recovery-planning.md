# Joint recovery tests, charging and accepted work

Recovery policy `scheduled-load-tests/2` connects the existing service candidate search to a common plant/dock/test optimisation. This is an opt-in operating controller, not a hypothetical repair effect. Select **Joint work, charging and tests for MPC (version 2)** in the recovery setup and **Shared visits and measured repair follow-up (version 3)** in service setup. Finite logistics, activity-based prices and sensing remain prerequisites. Greedy keeps the independent version-1 recovery scheduler and local service rule, explicitly recorded in provenance; comparisons span policy packages.

Each candidate projects accepted and proposed service work, isolation, stock use and robot return reserves. The same optimisation chooses process requests and dock charging while testing both declared delivery hypotheses. Before an observation distinguishes branches, their requested actions and dock occupancy/power are equal. Hypothetical failure can change delivered electrolyser power; it cannot give the controller private fault information. The ordinary and scenario planners reuse the same charging constraints and independent trajectory reconciliation.

The executive accepts the selected test window along with the current service decision. Future decisions preserve that absolute window, target and original episode deadline. An informative consecutive prefix consumes the remaining duration. Failed tracking, a changed observed capacity, a newly eligible procedure receipt or an unresolved continuation can release the window with a recorded reason. None silently extends the original deadline. A missed deadline is recorded before a later retry episode can start. Physical limits still take priority; a missing joint incumbent uses the existing feasible essential-work fallback and leaves the test unresolved.

Robot consumption requires beginning-of-interval inventory plus its declared return reserve. Charging credits arrive at interval end. One robot occupies the dock in each hour, subject to availability and the forecast solar limit. A plant battery can supply process loads while solar supplies the dock; the dock cannot charge in an interval without forecast solar. The exact pooled incremental dock cost is included once. Fixed ownership allocation remains outside dispatch incentives. Terminal plant energy reserves and original robot energy deadlines are unchanged.

Recorded service alternatives preserve the accepted test while changing a compatible work schedule or charging target. Process alternatives replan dock power against the original fixed service forecast and charging inputs, replacing the old dock load rather than adding it twice. Source runs remain immutable; an incompatible alternative remains unresolved. Older test archives retain the existing service-alternative limitation when their original joint inputs were not recorded.

The Site services pane reveals **Reserved recovery test** only when relevant. It shows the accepted window, load, original deadline and interruption reasons. The main plant illustration is unchanged. The Model controller essay distinguishes this operating scheduler from speculative future tests inside the inspection comparison. Post-intervention belief transitions, adoption of an entire contingent investigation plan, and a live investigation-selection what-if remain separate unfinished work.

The numerical example in `tests/test_joint_recovery.py` supplies 270, 270 and 10 kW across three hours. A 270 kW two-interval test is accepted at hour 0. Charging the empty rover with 10 kWh in hour 0 blocks it. Joint planning moves charging to hour 2: the rover receives 8 kWh, loses 2 kWh, meets its original hour-3 boundary and incurs the illustrative €3 dock allowance. Both delivery hypotheses share the request and charging plan. This demonstrates a bounded coordination opportunity, not a general methane advantage.
