# Recorded service economics

Two price versions are supported. Version 1 remains the default for compatibility. Opt-in `dispatch-lab/service-economics/2` derives each priced asset's active time from its original mission interval events, including fixed readers, reset devices and chargers. It rejects missing or overlapping intervals rather than reconstructing activity from a picture or assuming zero. Crew time remains separately metered. Selecting this version is an explicit frozen assumption; it does not rewrite older reports. A 1-hour fixed reading plus 0.25-hour verification at €8 per active hour produces €10 in version 2; the older counters omit fixed-reader activity.

Candidate pricing uses the same arithmetic with predicted quantities clearly separated from observed events. It subtracts the cost of the recorded prefix and existing commitments from the cost with added work. For example, with a €100 brush already in the usage/parts pool, two new hours at €10/hour add zero to that pool; twelve add €20. Fixed ownership does not enter this incremental dispatch cost. Unknown supply acceptance or work continuing beyond the priced boundary leaves the complete decision cost conditional, even when part of the expenditure is known. See [service scheduling](service-planning-ports.md).

`dispatch-lab/service-economics/1` reads recorded finite-logistics mission/resource events. An optional `Config.service_economics` freezes its complete assumptions once per run. Plant allocation, the revealed service inspector, recorded calculation traces, Model and exports use that selected ledger. `reprice_run` also produces a standalone report edition. Both substitute the service subtotal exactly once; neither changes actions or overwrites the source archive. [Generalised field Studies](field-studies.md) now preserve original calculations for matched hardware packages. Scheduled maintenance execution and standby-demand mechanics remain required work; the accounting adapter alone does not complete stage 3.

Three views are kept separate:

- **Allocated period cost:** body ownership, site preparation, mapping, access, payloads/references, standing services, consumable use, rejected procurement and a replaceable allowance.
- **Action-dependent costs:** recorded crew/remote time, vehicle operating time, callouts, consumable use, replacement/usage allowance and active contracted service. Fixed ownership, retainers and standing subscriptions are excluded.
- **Modelled service expenditure:** assumed billings and procurement at recorded upstream release. Pre-existing equipment and initial stocks are not new purchases unless their explicit opening-purchase flags are enabled. This is not a claim about invoices, payment timing or total plant cash flow.

The current fixture assumes continuous billing for standing service charges. Scheduled-maintenance charges exclude the separately recorded labour and parts; charging an allowance does not simulate the maintenance visit. Real billing calendars and additional maintenance tasks require their own model. Procurement is paid on upstream release, with no credit for rejected material in this fixture. Consumption from an existing store affects allocation/decision costs but is not another purchase. Opening and ending stores remain visible without sale proceeds.

Crew costs use the actual finite `crew-hours` ledger, including travel, setup, hands-on work, return and transfer. The old hands-on scalar is not added again. Vehicle cost per recorded travel hour excludes the crew. The model does not infer travel distance from the illustration. Remote time is separate, and a bundled itinerary retains its actual single departure callout. These choices make human dependence and partly shared visits visible without manufacturing a discount.

An owned asset excludes its replaceable subset from body ownership. The replaceable allocation is the maximum of calendar allowance, usage proxy and compatible parts consumed; the decision view excludes the calendar term. Part procurement remains in the separate expenditure view. Individual operands are retained. The reference cleaner pool covers its configured brush and drive parts; rover/dock pools cover their configured hardware modules. These are illustrative cost pools, not calibrated failure-rate or component-life estimates.

Contracted/shared provision explicitly includes equipment ownership, payload, references, replacement wear/parts and scheduled maintenance. It therefore cannot also charge these as owned hardware. Site-specific installation, mapping, access, external consumables and human/vehicle work stay separate. Shared provision alone does not model other clients, availability conflicts or a marketplace; those must be present in the physical schedule for comparative conclusions.

Every price is editable in the complete JSON assumption document. `illustrative(Costs())` retains existing numeric defaults where applicable and labels additional starting values as assumptions. It supplies no vendor quote or empirical evidence. An applicable `null` rate leaves the affected total undefined and exposes the known subtotal and missing term. It never silently means zero. Unsupported ledger versions remain explicitly unsupported by this adapter while their original reports remain available.

The three service totals and the combined plant report exclude a second electricity bill: service electricity already competes on the plant bus. They also exclude a duplicate lost-production penalty, since methane consequences are in the physical trace. The current report interface accepts a contiguous interval prefix from hour zero; it rejects an interior window rather than inventing its opening inventory or acquisition timing.

Generate an immutable, offline report with:

```sh
.venv/bin/python -m methane.service_economics path/to/archive.json.gz --assumptions path/to/prices.json --directory build/services/economics/reports
```

Omitting `--assumptions` uses the explicitly illustrative fixture. Each saved report retains the source run, original dispatch-price identity, current report-price identity, execution-source identity, calculation operands and source paths. Its JSON is the machine-readable companion to a self-contained amber/Departure Mono report. A different report edition does not replace the old file.

Tests compare crew, procurement, rejected water, contract coverage and replacement allowances with independently specified decimal expectations. Recorded recovery cases check that repricing leaves the complete trace unchanged. These numerical checks do not establish economic attractiveness or validate the illustrative prices. Those questions remain for the six matched field-study questions in the roadmap.

Saving an economic edition also preserves its loaded pricing source capsule beside the report, named by the capsule's SHA-256 identity. The report binds that exact capsule. Later code cannot silently regenerate the same edition using a different implementation; the original source must be restored. The original physical archive remains separate, with its own execution source and integrity identity.

In experiment setup, **Use complete service accounting** reveals work/material rates and one asset tab at a time. Prices, ownership life, installation, mapping, access, payloads/references and provision assumptions are editable. Switching to contracted/shared provision sets the covered ownership/wear inputs to zero; switching back requires a purchase price. That user-input action does not run on archive restoration. Reports keep unknown prices explicitly undefined. The main simulation remains unchanged until costs or site services are revealed.

Hiding this editor preserves its selected asset and unsaved values. Empty numeric prices show **Unpriced**, including when a field is rendered again; no empty price becomes zero. Invalid price combinations show the violated condition before replacing the cost report. On narrow screens, the tab overflow menu exposes every asset.

Each decision records the original service-price identity separately from the existing plant dispatch-price identity. The current local service rule does not optimise these new costs; it records its fixed economic assumptions for the later joint-planning stage. Repricing carries a separate service-report price identity through the player, recorded trace and Model transport, including an explicit switch back to the earlier reporting model. Late requests remain keyed to the current run/controller/hour and cost revision. Numerical accounting stays in Python; detailed lines are loaded on inspection, while playback frames carry compact summaries.

New reproduction bundles include `service-economics.html`, `service-economics.json` and the pricing source capsule when this model is selected. Repriced service ZIP exports use distinct report filenames. The standard-library checker independently recalculates service allocation, decision costs and modelled expenditure with Decimal arithmetic, and checks the original price identity carried by each decision. Old archives retain their original configuration and numerical semantics.
