# Portable cleaning, water and operator work

`portable-cleaning/1` is a contracted, operator-assisted tool assembled through the existing service registry. Choose `portable_cleaner = "dry"` or `"wet"` with `section-optical/1`, `logistics/1` and an enabled contracted crew. The runtime is `plant-service-contracts/4`; the surface treatment identity becomes `array-surface/2`. Older dry-brush runs keep their original source, timing and column meanings.

The tool travels with one crew from the declared service origin. Its mission reserves that crew for outbound travel, setup, treatment, visual verification and return. Each actual phase consumes crew allowance. The whole visit must fit the shift and response lead; an aborted portable mission also makes its operator unavailable for another visit. The portable and resident cleaners share exclusive work-area reservations for each array section.

The illustrative defaults are 1,000 m²/hour coverage, half an hour of setup, 0.5 kW while treating, 90% removal of loose material, and 70% removal of adhered material for the wet method. Wet work requires 0.5 litres/m² and 10 litres of setup rinsing. Setup rinsing consumes water at a constant rate during setup; it does not treat panel area. The verification stage stops the pump. Initial on-site water is 1,000 litres, with a 1,000-litre store, 5,000 litres in the upstream pipeline and 1,000-litre delivery lots. All are editable assumptions, not vendor specifications or a field-calibrated operating procedure.

For each covered strip, the wet model calculates `loose_after = loose_before × (1 − loose_removal)` and `adhered_after = adhered_before × (1 − adhered_removal)`. Damage is unchanged. Dry treatment has zero adhered removal. Untreated area keeps its former state. The existing optical model computes area-weighted transmission from the product of the three remaining factors, before converter clipping. Electricity recovered at the array is distinct from electricity used by the plant and methane produced.

Hourly execution intersects the fractional work schedule, consuming the corresponding water, tool electricity and labour. Partial work is committed at the next plant decision boundary. A failure retains covered area and spent water; it cannot silently finish the section or restore the tool. In this version a stranded portable tool/operator requires external assistance; a compatible automatic assistance procedure remains part of the unfinished support-hardware work. No universal repair capability is inferred from a cleaner illustration.

Wet eligibility checks the current ambient observation against an illustrative 5°C minimum, along with declared access and wind/rain limits. Missing current ambient or required weather blocks work. Ambient eligibility is not a panel thermal-shock model. The controller receives only current observations and the explicitly ideal surface monitor; it has no access to future weather or the seeded future mission outcome. Neither brush removes snow, repairs damaged cells, certifies washing chemistry or simulates contact force, scratches, runoff or water recycling.

The local portable rule requests a section when its compatible loose/adhered threshold is exceeded. A full section's setup and treatment resources must be available; missing water never converts a wet request into a dry one or silently reduces its area. A water delivery uses a separate crew visit and the finite upstream ledger. Water stays in litres throughout reservation, consumption and delivery; accepted and rejected excess are recorded at the receipt boundary. This deliberately exposes the need for the later visit-bundling and joint service-planning work.

Existing operator work, visit and material-kit prices are allocated once. Portable tool hire, cleaning-water procurement and rejected-water quantities are explicitly unpriced pending stage 3's complete cost assumptions. The tool is not charged as a permanently resident robot. Production loss appears in the physical trace, not as a second monetary subtraction. These short mechanism examples are not a cost-benefit ranking or an annual installation study.

The illustration adds a contracted service vehicle, a wheeled pump/tool unit, an operator, a brush lance and a hose. Sweep position follows recorded work progress. Water marks appear only while the wet method is working; reduced motion freezes the boundary pose. Geometry and tool reach are schematic and do not assert additional simulated mechanics. Existing plant equipment paths and labels remain unchanged.

Independent checks use decimal surface partitioning and optical multiplication, and separately derive setup water, treatment dose, coverage and crew duration from recorded stage intervals. Tests cover empty stock, dry/wet compatibility, cold conditions, crew unavailability, shared surface reservations, partial failure, conserved delivery volume and forged counters. These checks establish consistency of the declared mechanism, not empirical cleaning efficacy. Service-specific interactive Model essays, full running costs and published comparisons remain programme deliverables.
